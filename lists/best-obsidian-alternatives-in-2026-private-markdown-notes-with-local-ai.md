## 2. Logseq

**What you get.** A free, open-source outliner under AGPL-3.0 that calls itself privacy-first. Every line is a block you can link and reference, with daily journal pages built in.

**Best for.** People who think in outlines and daily journals.

**The catch.** Logseq is moving to a database version, and 2.0.2 is a beta of it. Its README warns that data loss is possible in the database beta.

## 3. Zettlr

**What you get.** A free Markdown editor for Windows, Mac and Linux under GPL-3.0. Its site says "all files stay on your computer." It has note IDs, wiki-style links and a graph view, and it works with Zotero and JabRef for citations.

**Best for.** Academic and long-form writers who cite sources.

**The catch.** Its site lists no built-in AI. Grammar checks come through LanguageTool.

## 4. Joplin

**What you get.** An open-source note app for Windows, Mac, Linux, Android and iOS, with a Markdown or rich-text editor and end-to-end encrypted sync. Joplin Cloud starts at €2.40 a month billed yearly.

**Best for.** People who want the same notes on every phone and computer, with sync they can trust.

**The catch.** Its AI, Joplin Cloud AI, is in beta and uses a chat model hosted by Joplin Cloud, so your notes go to that service when you use it.
