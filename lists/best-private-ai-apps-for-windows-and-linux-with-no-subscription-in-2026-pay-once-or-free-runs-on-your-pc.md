## Pick by the job you want done

### Chat with a private model

Every app in the table except Handy and Screenpipe does this for free. [Jan](https://github.com/janhq/jan) says it "runs 100% offline on your computer" and ships Windows, Linux .deb, AppImage and Flatpak builds. [LM Studio](https://lmstudio.ai/docs/app/system-requirements) runs on Windows and Linux, x64 or ARM, and needs AVX2 on x64. [GPT4All](https://github.com/nomic-ai/gpt4all) adds LocalDocs for chatting with your files, but its Linux build is x86-64 only. [Ollama](https://ollama.com/pricing) says running models on your own hardware "is always unlimited", and it's a runtime with an API, so it suits you if you live in a terminal.

Off Grid AI's free app also makes images on your GPU, speaks answers out loud and answers from your documents, using CUDA on NVIDIA cards and Vulkan on AMD and Intel. Its OpenAI-compatible API listens on port 7878 with keys off by default, so turn on **Require an API key** in **Gateway** before you share a Wi-Fi network.

### Meeting notes without a bot

Off Grid AI Pro records Zoom, Meet and Teams calls with system audio and your mic, spots the call on its own, and writes the transcript on your PC with whisper.cpp. Nobody sees a bot join, and the recording stays on your disk. That's the same on Windows and Linux in 0.0.56.

[Screenpipe](https://docs.screenpipe.com/) also transcribes audio locally and has meeting features. Its free plan keeps a limited history on one device, and the full history is $21 a month or $250 a year, so you're back to a subscription.

### Dictation into any app

[Handy](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy) is free, MIT licensed and works on Windows and Linux. On Linux it needs `xdotool` on X11, and its README calls Wayland support "limited", with `wtype` or `dotool` for typing.

Off Grid AI Pro's **Voice** works with **Alt+Space** on Windows and Linux. Hold it to talk or tap it to toggle, and the text lands at your cursor. It keeps every recording in a searchable library, and you can drop in any audio or video file to get a transcript.

### Screen memory: find what you saw

Off Grid AI Pro's **Replay** saves your screen as you work and lets you scrub back to the doc or number you saw. A local vision model reads the frames on Windows and Linux, and by default the heavy analysis runs overnight, from 03:00 to 08:00. On GNOME with Wayland, the default on Ubuntu, the app asks you to turn on its **Off Grid AI Capture** extension so it can see which window is in focus.

[Screenpipe](https://screenpipe.com/) runs on macOS, Windows and Linux and keeps "raw history" local by default. Its own docs say cloud transcription, AI providers and sync "can send selected data elsewhere", so check which you turn on.

### Time tracking with no timers

Off Grid AI Pro's **Reflect** shows where your day went, by app, project and person, built from the screen history Replay already saved. It's computed on your PC and listed for Windows and Linux in 0.0.56. None of the other apps on this list sells itself as a time tracker.

### Computer use: an assistant that does the clicks

Off Grid AI Pro acts on websites and desktop apps with **Web Use** and **Computer Use**, with your approval, and the 0.0.56 build has Computer Use for both Windows and Linux. [LM Studio](https://lmstudio.ai/) advertises "computer control" in its Bionic agent, but its page offers only a Windows x64 download of Bionic and doesn't name Linux.
