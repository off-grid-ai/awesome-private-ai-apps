## 1. Off Grid AI

**What it is.** A free desktop AI app, and Pro adds capture. Choose **Resume capture** in **Replay** and OGAD saves moments from your screen as you work. Each moment is a screenshot plus the text on screen, at most once every 20 seconds per app, so there's no video file. A local model reads each moment and notes the app, the project and the people in it. Then **Reflect** shows your day or week by project, app and person, how often you switched, and your longest focus block. The **Day** view adds a timeline and a journal.

Capture stays off until you turn it on, and you can pause it, exclude apps or delete moments whenever you like. Your screen moments stay on your disk and aren't uploaded. You need internet once to download the models. If you'd rather keep your computer fast during work hours, you can hold the model work for a night window you pick.

**Best for.** Seeing your own time by person and project, privately, on Mac, Windows or Linux. The same moments feed Memory, so you can also ask about your past work.

**Price.** OGAD is free. Pro is $34.99 once, a limited-time price down from $119, or $7.99 a month, and covers up to 5 devices.

**The catch.** There are no timesheets, billing or distraction blocking. You need a computer that can run a small local model. The Mac build needs Apple silicon, and the Linux build needs x64 with Ubuntu 24.04 or newer. ## 2. ActivityWatch

**What it is.** A free, open-source tracker (MPL-2.0) that records the apps, window titles and websites you use. Its [site](https://activitywatch.net/) says your data is stored locally and never sent to any server, and you look at it in a local web page.

**Best for.** Anyone who wants a free tracker with the data on their own machine, on any desktop or on Android.

**Price.** Free, with no feature limits. Optional ActivityWatch Pro support plans start at $5 a month.

**The catch.** You get raw app and site time. Its pages don't list AI summaries or time by person, and sync between devices is still in development.

## 3. Memtime

**What it is.** An automatic tracker for people who bill by the hour. It logs the window in front, plus file names, email subjects and tab titles, on a daily timeline. Its [home page](https://www.memtime.com/) says activity data is "stored exclusively on the user's device".

**Best for.** Freelancers and firms who turn their day into billable entries. It has 100+ integrations.

**Price.** From its [pricing page](https://www.memtime.com/pricing): Basic $14, Connect $21 and Premium $29 a user a month, billed yearly. There's no monthly plan, and there's a 14-day free trial.

**The catch.** Your project data lives in Memtime's web platform, and activity data doesn't sync between devices. Its pages don't list AI features.

## 4. Timing

**What it is.** A Mac time tracker with strong reports. Its [privacy page](https://timingapp.com/privacy) says your activity isn't sent to Timing unless you use Sync.

**Best for.** Mac users who bill clients and want detailed reports.

**Price.** AI summaries start on Expert, $11 a month billed yearly, per [Timing's blog](https://timingapp.com/blog/mac-time-tracking-apps/). Connect is $168 a year on its [pricing page](https://timingapp.com/pricing). There's a 30-day free trial.

**The catch.** It's macOS only. The AI summaries run on Google's Vertex AI in the EU, and Sync copies aren't end-to-end encrypted. See [Off Grid AI vs Timing](https://getoffgridai.co/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-timing-2026-ai-time-summaries-made-on-your-computer-not-in-the-cloud/).

## 5. RescueTime

**What it is.** A long-running tracker built around habits. Its [privacy page](https://www.rescuetime.com/rtx/privacy) lists app names, window titles and trimmed URLs, and says it never collects keystrokes or screenshots.

**Best for.** Changing your habits. Focus Sessions block distractions, and there are phone apps.

**Price.** From its [pricing page](https://www.rescuetime.com/pricing): Solo is $9 a month or $84 a year, and Solo+ with timesheets is $15 a month or $144 a year.

**The catch.** Your activity is stored on RescueTime's side, with volume encryption.

## 6. Rize

**What it is.** A timer-free tracker that writes your time entries with AI. Its [privacy page](https://rize.io/privacy) says activity metadata goes to Gemini, Anthropic and OpenAI, and the optional Vision feature reads what's on your screen.

**Best for.** Teams and freelancers who want AI-written entries and billing reports on any laptop.

**Price.** From its [pricing page](https://rize.io/pricing): Basic is $12.99 a month or $9.99 billed yearly, and Pro is $28.99 or $23.99. There's a 7-day trial and no free tier.

**The catch.** Your data is hosted on AWS, and AI features use monthly credits. Linux is "coming soon". See [Off Grid AI vs Rize](https://getoffgridai.co/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-rize-2026-time-tracking-that-never-uploads-your-day/).

## 7. Timely

**What it is.** A team timesheet tool. Its Memory app records the apps and sites you use, then you drag those memories into your timesheet. Its [privacy page](https://www.timely.com/privacy/) says tracked activity is uploaded to your Memory timeline, which only you can see.

**Best for.** Agencies that want AI timesheets, budgets and capacity planning in one place.

**Price.** From its [pricing page](https://www.timely.com/pricing): Starter is $11 a user a month or $9 billed yearly, and Premium is $20 or $16. There's a 14-day free trial.

**The catch.** Your activity is stored on AWS servers in Europe. Its [downloads page](https://www.timely.com/downloads/) lists macOS 14 and Windows 10 or later, and no Linux.
