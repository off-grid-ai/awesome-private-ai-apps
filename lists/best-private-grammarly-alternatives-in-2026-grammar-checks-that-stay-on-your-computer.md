## 2. Harper

**What you get.** A free, open-source grammar checker from Automattic under the Apache-2.0 licence. Its site says "Every check happens locally. No cloud round-trips, no telemetry, no LLM in the loop." There's a Harper Desktop app for macOS that works across text fields, extensions for Chrome, Edge and Firefox, and plugins for Obsidian, VS Code, Neovim, Zed and WordPress.

**Best for.** Free, fast English grammar checks, especially for developers and Obsidian users.

**The catch.** English only. It checks and suggests fixes but doesn't rewrite with AI.

## 3. Apple Writing Tools

**What you get.** Built into macOS with Apple Intelligence. Select text, Control-click and choose **Writing Tools** to proofread, rewrite in a Friendly, Professional or Concise tone, or summarize. Apple says it works in most places you write, including third-party apps and websites.

**Best for.** Mac owners who want proofreading with nothing to install.

**The catch.** Mac only, and only on Macs with Apple Intelligence (M1 or later). Larger requests go to Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which Apple says doesn't store your data, and Apple says usage limits may apply.

## 4. Writing Tools (open source)

**What you get.** A free app under GPL-3.0 for Windows, macOS 14 and later, and Linux. Select text in any app and press **Ctrl+Space** to proofread or rewrite it. You choose the AI behind it: cloud services such as Gemini or OpenAI, or a local model through Ollama or llama.cpp.

**Best for.** Tinkerers who already run a local model server and want a free hotkey rewriter.

**The catch.** It's only private if you set up a local model yourself. Its README calls Linux a work in progress with Wayland caveats, and its latest release was 10 May 2026. It works on the text you select, when you press the hotkey.

## 5. LanguageTool

**What you get.** A grammar checker for many languages with browser add-ons and apps for Mac and Windows. The free plan checks up to 2,000 characters per text field and gives 3 AI rephrasings a day. It says texts written through the browser add-on are "usually not saved."

**Best for.** Writing in languages other than English.

**The catch.** It checks in LanguageTool's cloud by default. You can run it as a local server with Java 8 or later (LGPL-2.1), but its docs say the AI-based rules only work in the cloud, and the Windows desktop app can't use the local server.

## FAQ

**What is the best private alternative to Grammarly?**
Off Grid AI Pro. Its Scribe feature checks spelling and grammar in every app on Mac, Windows and Linux, rewrites with a model on your own computer and follows your style guide, for one payment. Harper is the best free pick if you only need checks in English.

**Does Grammarly send my writing to its servers?**
Yes. Grammarly's privacy policy, now published by Superhuman, says it processes your user content to give writing suggestions. The checkers ranked here can do the work on your own computer instead.

**Is there a free grammar checker that works offline?**
Harper. It's free, open source under Apache-2.0 and runs every check on your device, with a Mac app, browser extensions and editor plugins. It covers English only and doesn't rewrite with AI.

**Can I run LanguageTool locally?**
Yes. LanguageTool can run as a local server on your computer with Java 8 or later. Its AI-based rules only work in LanguageTool's cloud, so the local version checks with its standard rules.

**Does Apple Writing Tools work offline?**
Partly. Apple Intelligence runs on your Mac where it can, and sends larger requests to Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which Apple says doesn't store your data. It needs a Mac with Apple Intelligence, M1 or later.
